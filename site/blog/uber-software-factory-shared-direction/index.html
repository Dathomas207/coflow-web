Industry direction

Uber’s software factory points to a broader direction for AI development.

Shared context, reviewed workflows and outcome-based measurement are becoming part of the engineering platform around coding agents.

By CoFlow · November 20, 2026 · 3 min read

In this articleWhat Uber has publishedThe direction: agents supported by an engineering systemWhere CoFlow fitsApply the principle at your scale

The conversation is moving beyond which coding agent writes the best first draft. The next question is how a team makes those agents effective inside its own codebase, workflows and engineering standards.

Uber’s public engineering work is a useful example of this direction. It shows why the systems around an agent deserve as much attention as the model generating the code.

What Uber has published

In Running a Software Factory Efficiently at Uber Scale, published August 27, 2026, Uber describes internal platforms and managed agents spanning development, review and maintenance. The article explains its work on shared organizational context, reusable skills, model selection and cost visibility.

Uber also describes measuring completed outcomes and quality alongside model usage. Its reported efficiency improvements belong to Uber’s environment. They should not be treated as expected results for CoFlow or another team.

The direction: agents supported by an engineering system

Our reading of Uber’s experience is that a model alone is not the whole development platform. Agents need relevant context, a dependable path through the work and a way to assess the result.

DORA’s analysis of AI adoption points in a similar direction: internal platforms, clear workflows and testing practices shape how well teams turn faster generation into useful delivery.

These examples show a growing direction across engineering organizations. They do not establish that every team is adopting the same architecture. The common opportunity is to make AI-assisted development repeatable, understandable and accountable.

Where CoFlow fits

CoFlow focuses on the reviewed engineering knowledge that should accompany a coding task: approved standards, relevant lessons, scoped instructions, acceptance requirements and recorded outcomes.

  • Review the knowledge before it becomes guidance.
  • Compile relevant instructions for the task and preserve the package revision.
  • Connect requirements and outcomes so the team can examine what happened.
  • Bring context into existing workflows through the documented MCP and export paths.

CoFlow is not Uber’s internal platform and does not claim its full execution, routing or infrastructure capabilities. Our focus is helping teams bring their own engineering knowledge into the agents they already use.

Apply the principle at your scale

You can start with one repository and one recurring source of rework. Give the next task a reviewed rule and a meaningful acceptance check. Record the result. Expand when the evidence shows that the guidance is helping.

The case for this direction is practical: as generating code gets easier, knowing what to build, which standards apply and how to evaluate the result becomes more valuable.
