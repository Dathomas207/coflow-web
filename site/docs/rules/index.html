Rules, lessons and human review

How evidence becomes a proposal, and how a person turns a proposal into a rule.

Rules live in your repository

A rule is a file under .coflow/rules/, versioned with your code. It names where it applies, how it is checked, its severity and its examples. coflow sync posts the repository’s rules to the hosted service so server review and the hosted MCP server use the same set.

Where lessons come from

  • Review findings and how people disposed of them.
  • Failed checks, reverts and fixes of earlier fixes.
  • Tickets in Jira or Linear, and production incidents and error groups from your monitoring tools.
  • Corrections people make in coding-agent sessions, captured by a session-end hook or recorded with coflow lesson.

The lessons pipeline

The pipeline classifies evidence, clusters related items, drafts a rule, checks it against existing rules so a lesson is stored once, and opens a proposal with its evidence attached. Before any of that, secrets are dropped, identifiers are replaced and instruction-like text is neutralized. Mined text is data, never instructions.

Drafting and testing a lesson with a model are credit-using work under the planned billing model.

Human review

  • A proposal is never active. A person reviews it, and the rule takes effect when its pull request is merged.
  • Approval policies cover new rules, rule changes, retirements and deviations. Each names its reviewers and approvers and how many of each are needed.
  • The person who proposed a rule cannot approve it while another eligible reviewer exists. A proposal made by an agent counts as proposed by the member who created the agent’s credential.
  • If the author is the only eligible reviewer, the record says “approved by the only eligible reviewer” and marks independence as not met.
  • Reviewers can decide in the app or on a Slack or Teams card. A chat decision is recorded; the rule still becomes active only when its pull request merges.

See organizations, roles and approvals for policies, quorum and independence.

Rules and facts are different

A rule constrains behavior and takes effect only after a person approves it. A fact about your project, such as where a database is or how to run a service, is remembered as soon as an agent learns it. See project memory.
